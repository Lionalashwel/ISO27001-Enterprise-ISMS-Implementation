## 1. Project Overview

This project presents an Information Security Management System (ISMS) designed for Al-Afaq Digital Bank. The project translates a banking security scenario into a structured ISO/IEC 27001:2022 implementation package.

The bank provides digital banking services, including the Afaq Plus application, customer and transaction databases, international transfers, fraud-monitoring services, branches, ATMs, data centers, disaster-recovery facilities, cloud services, and technology suppliers.

The project addresses the protection of information confidentiality, integrity, and availability. It also demonstrates how an organization can identify its context, define the ISMS scope, inventory its information assets, assess and treat risks, select applicable controls, establish security objectives, operate security processes, measure performance, conduct internal audits, and prepare for continual improvement.

> **Important status note:** Some documents in the supporting evidence package are explicitly prepared as educational simulations and templates. They must not be represented as real production records unless the team actually performs and signs the corresponding tests or audit activities.

---

## 2. Project Objectives

The project is intended to:

1. Define a practical ISMS scope for a digital banking environment.
2. Establish an information security policy and governance direction.
3. Identify information assets, owners, classifications, and dependencies.
4. Assess information security risks using a documented methodology.
5. Select and justify applicable ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls.
6. Establish measurable information security objectives.
7. Define secure IT operating procedures and incident response activities.
8. Plan business continuity and disaster recovery for critical banking services.
9. Monitor ISMS performance and prepare internal-audit evidence.
10. Demonstrate audit readiness through an external-audit simulation, traceability matrix, corrective-action plan, team records, and presentation.

## 5. Core ISMS Documents

The following fifteen documents form the main ISO/IEC 27001 implementation package. They must be kept together and must not be replaced by the supporting evidence files.

### 5.1 ISMS Scope Document

**File:** `ISMS Scope Document.docx`

Defines the ISMS boundaries, covered banking services, locations, technologies, departments, suppliers, interfaces, dependencies, interested parties, and exclusions with their rationale.

### 5.2 Information Security Policy

**File:** `Information Security Policy.docx`

Defines management commitment, security principles, responsibilities, compliance expectations, awareness requirements, incident reporting, review, and continual improvement.

### 5.3 Risk Assessment and Treatment Register

**File:** `Risk Assessment and Treatment.xlsx`

Provides the asset-based risk register, including affected assets, threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood, impact, risk ratings, treatment actions, owners, and status.

### 5.4 Statement of Applicability

**File:** `Statement of Applicability.xlsx`

Documents the applicability of Annex A controls, including inclusion or exclusion decisions, justifications, implementation status, and linked evidence.

### 5.5 Risk Assessment and Treatment Methodology

**File:** `Risk Assessment and Treatment Methodology.docx`

Explains the risk criteria, likelihood and impact scales, assessment process, treatment options, risk acceptance, residual risk, review responsibilities, and risk appetite.

### 5.6 List of Security Objectives

**File:** `List of Security Objectives.docx`

Defines measurable objectives for vulnerability management, encryption, access review, awareness, incident response, backup success, and other security priorities.

### 5.7 Risk Assessment and Treatment Report

**File:** `Risk Assessment and Treatment Report.docx`

Summarizes the most significant banking risks, treatment decisions, residual risks, management responsibilities, priorities, and progress.

### 5.8 Information Asset Inventory

**File:** `Information Asset Inventory.xlsx`

Lists the bank's information and technology assets, owners, locations, classifications, criticality, confidentiality, integrity, availability, dependencies, and protection status.

### 5.9 Acceptable Use Policy

**File:** `Acceptable Use Policy.docx`

Defines acceptable and prohibited use of bank devices, networks, email, applications, cloud services, removable media, and credentials.

### 5.10 Information Security Incident Response Procedure

**File:** `Information Security Incident Response Procedure.docx`

Defines incident detection, reporting, classification, analysis, containment, eradication, recovery, evidence preservation, escalation, communication, and lessons learned.

### 5.11 Internal Audit Report

**File:** `Internal Audit Report.docx`

Documents the internal-audit scope, criteria, evidence reviewed, findings, nonconformities, observations, corrective actions, owners, and due dates.

### 5.12 Security Operating Procedures for IT Management

**File:** `Security Operating Procedures for IT Management.docx`

Covers backup and recovery, patching, vulnerability management, access administration, logging and monitoring, change management, supplier operations, and secure daily operation.

### 5.13 ISMS Performance Report

**File:** `ISMS Performance Report.docx`

Reports ISMS performance indicators, including vulnerability closure, backup success, security awareness, access reviews, incident response, corrective actions, and management-review decisions.

### 5.14 Security Configuration Standard

**File:** `Security Configuration Standard.docx`

Defines secure configuration baselines for servers, databases, network devices, endpoints, cloud services, authentication, privileged accounts, encryption, and logging.

### 5.15 Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Plan

**File:** `Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Plan.docx`

Defines critical services, recovery priorities, emergency roles, communication channels, disaster recovery at the Jeddah site, RTO, RPO, testing, failover, and return to normal operations.

Links each scenario gap to an affected asset, risk, control, document, security objective, performance indicator, owner, and verification evidence. It also provides an index of the fifteen core documents.

### 6.2 External Audit Plan

**File:** `02_External_Audit_Plan.docx`

Defines the purpose, scope, criteria, schedule, roles, evidence requirements, and evaluation rules for the external-audit simulation.

### 6.3 External Audit Report

**File:** `03_External_Audit_Report.docx`

The additional files were added to strengthen the project evaluation in the following areas:

- **Documentation completeness:** An index and evidence package make the submission easier to review.
- **Logical traceability:** The traceability matrix connects gaps, assets, risks, controls, objectives, and verification evidence.
- **Practical applicability:** Test scenarios show how the documented processes can be executed.
- **Audit simulation:** The external-audit plan, report, corrective-action plan, and checklist prepare the team for auditor questions.
- **Teamwork assessment:** The team log and meeting minutes document role allocation, peer review, and participation.
- **Presentation readiness:** The slide deck gives the team a concise structure for the final discussion.

These additions were made to increase project readiness and support a high evaluation. They do not guarantee a specific academic grade. The final grade also depends on the quality of the team's presentation, discussion, and actual performance during the simulation.

## 9. Important Evidence and Integrity Statement

The test evidence and external-audit records are identified as educational simulations unless the team performs the activities in a real or controlled environment. The project must not claim that a backup restore, disaster-recovery failover, ransomware exercise, or external audit occurred in production if it did not.

Recommended wording during presentation:

> “These records are simulation evidence prepared to demonstrate the expected execution method. Actual operational evidence should be attached after the corresponding activity is performed.”

This distinction protects the integrity of the submission and demonstrates professional information-security practice.
